About this guide

Squamish Voters 2026 is an independent, non-partisan voter guide. It is not connected to the District of Squamish, any candidate, campaign, party or business interest.

Who made this

Jeff Kirdeikis, a Squamish resident, built and runs this site on his own time and pays for it himself. No candidate, campaign, party, business or organization asked for it, funds it, or sees it before it is published.

Questions, corrections or complaints go straight to Jeff:

What we did

  • We started from the District’s official list of declared candidates — all {{COUNT}} people running for mayor and council.
  • For each person we read their campaign website, local news coverage (mainly the Squamish Chief and the Squamish Reporter), letters and public statements. For current councillors we also used their 2022–2026 council voting record.
  • Every position on this site links to its source. Look for the small [source] links.
  • We never guess. If we could not find a public statement on an issue, we write “No public position found”. That is not a criticism of the candidate — many are still publishing their platforms.
  • Candidates are always listed alphabetically by last name, the same as the ballot.

How the quiz works

Each quiz statement is scored from “strongly disagree” (−2) to “strongly agree” (+2). We scored each candidate the same way, using only their public statements and votes — or, where a candidate has answered our questionnaire directly (marked ✓ Answered us directly), their own answers, which replace our reading of the record. Your match with a candidate is how close your answers are to theirs, averaged over the questions where both of you have an answer. Identical answers score full marks, one step apart scores two thirds, two steps apart one third, and three or more steps apart nothing — so answering “mixed” does not count as agreeing with someone who strongly agrees. Questions you mark as important count double. Matches based on only a few questions are pulled slightly toward 50%, so a candidate with a thin public record cannot top your list by chance.

“No opinion — don’t count this one” leaves a question out of your score entirely; “Mixed — I’m in the middle” is a real answer and is scored. If a candidate has a public position on fewer than 4 of the questions you answered, we do not give them a percentage at all, and list them separately. A high match on few questions is weaker than a high match on many — the results always show how many questions each match is based on.

Your shortlist only includes candidates you agree with more than you disagree with (a match of 55% or more). If fewer than 6 council candidates clear that bar, we show fewer rather than filling the list with people whose views are the opposite of yours. The others are still listed further down, marked “you mostly disagree”.

Your answers stay in your own browser. We do not collect, store or see them.

How “Where they lean” and the compass work

Every placement is arithmetic on the same sourced scores the quiz uses; none of it is a judgment call. Each line on the Where they lean page (homelessness & street safety, money, growth, the Woodfibre tax deal, climate) is fed by a fixed list of quiz statements — the page shows exactly which ones under “How this is worked out”. A candidate’s position on a line is the plain average of their scores on those statements.

“Clearly” needs an average of 1.2 or more and at least three scored statements; a strong score on a single statement only counts as a “lean”. Averages between −0.34 and 0.34 read “in the middle”. If a candidate has no score on any statement of a line, they are listed under “Haven’t said yet” rather than placed in the middle. A hollow marker means the placement rests on a single statement.

The two-way map uses the same method: left–right is the average of the money and spending statements (where a candidate has fewer than two, we fall back to our written estimate of their economic lean and draw the dot hollow, labelled as an estimate); up–down is the homelessness line. Candidates missing either axis are not placed.

The code is open source

Anyone can check the arithmetic. The whole site — the quiz code, the “where they lean” code, and the data file with every candidate’s scores and sources — is published at github.com/jeffkirdeikis/squamish-voters. The methodology document there walks through every formula, with the file and line it lives in. If you find a mistake, open an issue there or email us.

Privacy: what we count

We count how many people visit each page, so we know whether the guide is being used. We use Vercel Web Analytics for this. It uses no cookies, does not follow you to other websites, and cannot tell us who you are.

We also count how many times the quiz is started and finished, and how many times someone adds to, saves or shares a ballot — the number only. We count which parts of the guide get used: which topic is opened on the Compare page, how far through the quiz people get, and how often a source link or a candidate’s written comment is opened. We never receive your quiz answers, your matches or the names on your ballot. Those stay in your own browser.

We do not endorse anyone

This guide does not tell you who to vote for. The quiz shows who is closest to your views, based on the public record. Please read the profiles, go to an all-candidates meeting if you can, and make up your own mind.

Corrections

We want this to be accurate and fair to every candidate. If you are a candidate and want to update your profile, add your positions on any issue, answer the quiz statements or send a photo, email us — we will add it. If you are anyone else and something here is wrong, out of date or missing, please tell us and include a link to a public source. We will fix confirmed errors quickly and note the date of the last update at the bottom of every page.
